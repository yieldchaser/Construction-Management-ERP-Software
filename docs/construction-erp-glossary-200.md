# Construction & ERP Glossary (A–Z) — 200 Terms

Real, industry-grounded definitions for SiteFlow's audience — builders, contractors, subcontractors, and project management consultancies across India and the Gulf. No invented terminology, no fabricated figures.

---

## A

**Acceleration**
Compressing a project schedule beyond its original plan, usually by adding resources or working overtime, to recover lost time or meet an earlier deadline.

**Approval Workflow**
A configured sequence of sign-offs (e.g., site engineer → project manager → finance) a document must pass through before it becomes final, preventing any single person from unilaterally committing spend.

**As-Built Drawing**
A drawing revised to reflect exactly what was constructed on site, including deviations from the original design, used for handover and future reference.

**Attendance (Geofenced)**
A worker punch-in/punch-out recorded only when the device's GPS location falls inside a defined radius around the project site.

**Advance Payment**
An upfront sum paid to a contractor or vendor before work begins or goods are delivered, typically recovered in instalments against later bills.

**Aggregate**
Sand, gravel, or crushed stone mixed with cement and water to form concrete; one of the most commonly tracked bulk materials in a project's procurement and inventory records.

**Arbitration Clause**
A contract clause requiring disputes to be resolved by a neutral third-party arbitrator rather than through court litigation.

**Audit Trail**
A chronological, tamper-evident record of who created, edited, or approved a transaction or document, used for compliance and dispute resolution.

**Acceptance Test**
A formal test carried out to confirm that a completed system, equipment, or work item meets the agreed specification before it is accepted by the client.

**Access Control (Site)**
The set of physical and digital measures — gate passes, biometric checks, permission-based logins — restricting who can enter a site or access specific project data.

**Addendum**
A document issued to amend or clarify a tender or contract after its original issue, forming part of the binding contract documents once accepted.

**Asset Register**
A record of all fixed assets (equipment, vehicles, tools) owned or leased by a company, tracking value, location, and depreciation over time.

---

## B

**Bank Guarantee (BG)**
A guarantee issued by a bank on a contractor's behalf, assuring the client that a specified sum will be paid if the contractor fails to meet contractual obligations — commonly used in place of cash retention.

**Baseline**
The originally approved project schedule or budget, frozen at a point in time, used as the fixed reference against which actual progress or spend is measured.

**Batching Plant**
A facility (fixed or mobile) that mixes cement, aggregate, and water in controlled proportions to produce ready-mix concrete for a project.

**BBS (Bar Bending Schedule)**
A detailed list specifying the shape, size, and quantity of reinforcement steel bars required for a structural element, used to plan procurement and cutting.

**BOCW Cess**
Building and Other Construction Workers' Welfare Cess — a statutory levy (typically 1% of construction cost) in India funding welfare schemes for construction labour.

**BOQ (Bill of Quantities)**
An itemized list of all materials, labour, and work items required for a project, each with a quantity, unit rate, and total cost — the financial backbone of a construction contract.

**Budget Revision**
A formally logged change to an approved project budget, tracked with a reason and approval so the original and revised figures remain auditable side by side.

**Burn Rate**
The rate at which project budget is spent over time, tracked and compared against the planned spend curve to flag overspending early.

**Backcharge**
A cost deducted from a subcontractor's or vendor's payment to recover expenses the main contractor incurred on their behalf, such as rework or cleanup.

**Bank Reconciliation**
The process of matching a company's cashbook records against the bank statement to confirm they agree, and identifying and explaining any differences.

**Bid Bond**
A guarantee submitted with a tender ensuring the bidder will honour their offer if awarded the contract, forfeited if they withdraw or fail to sign.

---

## C

**Cash Position**
The net cash available to a project or company at a given point — receipts minus payments — distinct from accounting profit.

**Cashbook**
A ledger recording every cash and bank transaction for a project or company, reconciled against bank statements.

**Cash Flow Forecast**
A projection of expected cash inflows and outflows over a future period, used to anticipate funding gaps before they occur.

**Change Order**
See **Variation Order**.

**Client Retention (Defects Liability)**
The portion of retention money held back specifically to cover the defects liability period after handover, released once that period expires without unresolved defects.

**Completion Certificate**
A formal document issued by a client, architect, or authority confirming that a project (or defined phase) has been completed as per approved plans.

**Cost Code**
A short alphanumeric identifier assigned to a specific type of work or expense, letting costs across purchase orders, labour, and invoices be rolled up and compared against budget by category.

**Cost-Plus Contract**
A contract type where the contractor is reimbursed actual costs plus an agreed fee or percentage margin, as opposed to a fixed lump-sum price.

**Cost-to-Complete**
An estimate of the remaining cost required to finish a project or task from its current state, used alongside cost-to-date to forecast final project cost.

**CPM (Critical Path Method)**
A scheduling technique identifying the longest sequence of dependent tasks — the critical path — where any delay directly delays overall project completion.

**Credit Note**
A document issued by a vendor to reduce the amount a buyer owes them, without reversing the original invoice entirely.

**CRM Lead**
A prospective client or project opportunity logged and tracked through stages (enquiry, quotation, negotiation, won/lost) before it converts into a signed contract.

**Clash Detection**
The process of identifying conflicts between different building systems (structural, MEP, architectural) in the design or model before construction begins, avoiding costly rework.

**Commissioning**
The process of testing and verifying that installed systems and equipment operate as designed before a facility is handed over for use.

**Comparative Statement**
A side-by-side comparison of quotations received from multiple vendors for the same scope, used to justify vendor selection.

**Contingency**
A budget reserve set aside to cover unforeseen costs or risks that may arise during a project, distinct from the base estimate.

**Cost Overrun**
The amount by which actual project cost exceeds the originally approved budget.

---

## D

**Debit Note**
A document issued to reduce an amount owed to a vendor, commonly for rejected goods, short delivery, or a rate correction.

**Deduction (RA Bill)**
Any statutory or contractual amount withheld from a subcontractor's running-account bill before payment — commonly TDS, retention, or advance recovery.

**Defects Liability Period (DLP)**
A defined period after handover during which the contractor remains responsible for rectifying defects at no additional cost to the client, typically 6–12 months.

**Demobilization**
The process of withdrawing labour, equipment, and temporary facilities from a site once work is complete or a contract ends.

**Design-Build**
A project delivery method where a single entity holds contractual responsibility for both design and construction, as opposed to the client hiring separate design and construction firms.

**DPR (Daily Progress Report)**
A day-by-day record of work completed, manpower deployed, and materials consumed, typically logged by a site engineer or supervisor.

**Drawing Revision**
A new, formally issued version of a drawing that supersedes a previous one, numbered sequentially so all stakeholders reference the current version.

**Due Diligence**
The verification process (financial, legal, technical) carried out before entering a contract or acquiring a project, to confirm claims and identify risk.

**Delay Analysis**
A structured review of a project schedule to determine the cause, responsibility, and time impact of a delay, often used to support an extension of time claim.

**Depreciation**
The systematic reduction in the recorded value of an asset (equipment, vehicles) over its useful life, reflecting wear and obsolescence.

**Design Basis Report**
A document setting out the design assumptions, criteria, and standards adopted for a project, used as a reference throughout design and construction.

**Direct Cost**
Cost directly attributable to a specific work item — material, labour, and equipment used on that item — as opposed to overhead or indirect cost.

---

## E

**Earned Value (EV)**
The budgeted cost of work actually completed, used alongside planned value and actual cost to assess whether a project is ahead/behind schedule and under/over budget.

**Earthwork**
Excavation, grading, and filling operations that reshape the ground before foundation work begins, typically measured in cubic metres of cut and fill.

**Escalation Clause**
A contract clause allowing the contract price to be adjusted if input costs (steel, cement, labour) rise beyond an agreed threshold during the project.

**ESI (Employees' State Insurance)**
A statutory Indian social security scheme providing medical and cash benefits to employees earning below a defined wage threshold, funded through payroll contributions.

**Estimate**
A calculated prediction of project cost prepared before a firm BOQ or contract exists, used for feasibility and early budgeting.

**EMD (Earnest Money Deposit)**
A refundable deposit submitted by a bidder along with a tender, demonstrating serious intent to bid and forfeited under specified default conditions.

**Escrow Account**
A dedicated account held by a neutral third party into which project funds are deposited and released only when agreed conditions are met, commonly used to reassure buyers or lenders.

**Extension of Time (EOT)**
A formally granted extension to a contract's completion date, typically approved when delay is caused by factors outside the contractor's control.

---

## F

**Face Recognition Attendance**
A biometric attendance method verifying worker identity via a facial scan on a mobile device, reducing buddy-punching.

**Fast-Tracking**
A schedule compression technique that overlaps tasks normally performed sequentially, accepting added risk to save time.

**Final Bill**
The last bill raised by a contractor or subcontractor closing out the full value of a completed contract, distinct from interim RA bills.

**Float (Schedule)**
The amount of time a task can be delayed without affecting the project's overall completion date; tasks on the critical path have zero float.

**Force Majeure**
A contract clause excusing a party from performance obligations due to extraordinary events beyond their control (natural disasters, war, government action).

**FRP (Formwork/Reinforcement/Pour)**
The three-stage sequence for casting a reinforced concrete element: erecting formwork, fixing reinforcement, and pouring concrete.

**Fixed Price Contract**
A contract where the total price is agreed in advance and does not change regardless of the contractor's actual cost, placing cost-overrun risk on the contractor.

**Fleet Management**
The tracking and coordination of a company's vehicles and heavy equipment — utilization, fuel consumption, maintenance schedules, and location.

**Fund Flow**
The movement of money into and out of a project or company over time, tracked to ensure sufficient funds are available when payments fall due.

---

## G

**Gantt Chart**
A horizontal bar chart representing a project schedule, with each bar showing a task's start date, duration, and end date.

**GRN (Goods Receipt Note)**
A document created when materials physically arrive at site, recording quantity and condition received against a purchase order.

**GST (Goods and Services Tax)**
India's unified indirect tax on the supply of goods and services, applicable to construction materials, works contracts, and most vendor invoices.

**Guaranteed Maximum Price (GMP)**
A contract arrangement where the contractor guarantees a ceiling price, absorbing any cost overrun beyond that cap while typically sharing in savings below it.

**General Conditions of Contract (GCC)**
The standard, boilerplate terms of a construction contract covering obligations, payment, delay, and dispute mechanisms, distinct from project-specific special conditions.

**Gross Margin**
Revenue from a project or company minus direct costs, expressed as a percentage of revenue, before overhead and other indirect costs are deducted.

---

## H

**Handover**
The formal process of transferring a completed project or phase from the contractor to the client, typically with as-built drawings, warranties, and a snag list.

**Haversine Formula**
The formula used to calculate the distance between two GPS coordinates, underlying geofenced attendance checks.

**Hindrance**
An event or condition (client delay, design non-availability, access restriction) preventing the contractor from proceeding with work as planned, often the basis for an extension of time claim.

**Hoisting**
The vertical movement of materials, equipment, or personnel on site using cranes, hoists, or lifts.

**Hazard Identification (HAZID)**
A structured exercise to identify potential hazards on a site or task before work begins, feeding into risk assessments and safety plans.

**Hold Point**
A stage in a quality inspection plan where work must stop until an inspection or approval is completed, before proceeding to the next stage.

---

## I

**Indent**
An internal request raised by a site or department for materials to be procured, converted into a purchase order once approved.

**Interim Payment Certificate (IPC)**
A certificate issued periodically (often monthly) confirming the value of work completed and the amount due to the contractor for that period.

**Inventory Ageing**
An analysis of how long stock has sat in a warehouse without movement, used to flag slow-moving or obsolete material.

**IS Code**
An Indian Standard published by the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) specifying technical requirements for materials, design, or construction practice.

**Indemnity**
A contractual promise by one party to compensate the other for specified losses or damages, shifting risk between the parties.

**Inspection Test Plan (ITP)**
A document listing the inspections and tests required for a scope of work, specifying who performs each check and at what stage.

**Interest on Delayed Payment**
A charge applied when a payment is made later than the contractually agreed due date, calculated on the overdue amount.

---

## J

**Joint Measurement**
A quantity or measurement recorded and signed off jointly by the contractor and client on site, used as the agreed billing basis when field conditions differ from the estimate.

**Joint Venture (JV)**
An arrangement where two or more companies combine resources to jointly execute a project, sharing risk, cost, and profit under an agreed structure.

**Job Safety Analysis (JSA)**
A step-by-step breakdown of a specific task identifying hazards at each step and the controls needed before that task is carried out.

---

## K

**KPI (Key Performance Indicator)**
A quantifiable metric tracked to assess project or company health — e.g., % complete, open RFIs, cash position — typically surfaced on a dashboard.

**Key Money**
An informal or contractual upfront payment sometimes made to secure priority access to labour, material, or site possession — practice and legality vary by region.

**Kickoff Meeting**
The initial project meeting bringing together client, contractor, and consultant teams to align on scope, schedule, roles, and communication protocols before work begins.

---

## L

**Labour Contract**
An agreement engaging a subcontractor or labour supplier to provide manpower for a defined scope, typically billed per unit of work or per day.

**Lead Time**
The time elapsed between placing an order and receiving the material or service, a key input for procurement planning.

**Lien**
A legal right to hold or claim against property (or retained payment) until a debt or obligation is settled.

**Liquidated Damages (LD)**
A pre-agreed sum payable by the contractor to the client for each day (or week) of delay beyond the contractual completion date, in lieu of proving actual loss.

**Lookahead Schedule**
A short-horizon schedule (commonly a rolling 2–6 week window) drawn from the master schedule, used for near-term planning.

**LTIF (Lost Time Injury Frequency Rate)**
A standard safety metric measuring lost-time injuries per million (or 200,000) hours worked.

**Labour Productivity**
A measure of output per unit of labour input (e.g., square metres of shuttering per mason-day), used to benchmark crew efficiency across sites or periods.

**Landed Cost**
The total cost of a material once it arrives at site, including the purchase price plus freight, duties, and handling charges.

**Letter of Credit (LC)**
A bank-issued guarantee of payment to a seller on behalf of a buyer, commonly used in high-value or cross-border material procurement.

**Lump Sum Contract**
A contract where the contractor agrees to complete the entire defined scope for one fixed total price, regardless of actual quantities used.

---

## M

**Material Reconciliation**
Comparing material issued to site against material actually consumed in completed work, used to detect wastage or estimation errors.

**Milestone**
A significant point in a project schedule marking completion of a major phase, often tied to milestone-based payments.

**Mobilization**
The initial phase of a project where a contractor sets up site facilities, deploys initial resources, and prepares to begin work.

**Mobilization Advance**
An upfront payment made to help a contractor cover mobilization costs, recovered in instalments against later bills.

**MOM (Minutes of Meeting)**
A written record of discussion points, decisions, and action items from a project meeting, circulated to track accountability.

**MEP (Mechanical, Electrical, Plumbing)**
The collective term for a building's mechanical, electrical, and plumbing systems and the trades that install them.

**Manpower Planning**
Forecasting the number and trade-mix of workers required across the project timeline, matched against schedule demand to avoid shortfalls or idle crews.

**Margin**
The profit retained after all direct and indirect costs are deducted from revenue, usually expressed as a percentage.

**Master Schedule**
The top-level project schedule covering the full project duration and all major phases, from which detailed lookahead schedules are derived.

**Material Take-Off (MTO)**
The process of measuring drawings to derive the exact quantities of materials required for a scope of work, feeding into the BOQ and procurement plan.

**Method Statement**
A document describing the sequence of activities and safety precautions for carrying out a specific piece of work, submitted for approval before execution.

---

## N

**NCR (Non-Conformance Report)**
A formal record raised when work, material, or a process fails to meet a specified quality standard, documenting root cause and corrective action.

**Novation**
The legal transfer of a contract's rights and obligations from one party to another with the consent of all parties.

**Net Payable**
The final amount due to a vendor or subcontractor after all deductions (TDS, retention, advance recovery) have been applied to the gross bill value.

**Negative Variation**
A variation order that reduces the original contract scope or value, as opposed to the more common additive variation.

**Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA)**
A legal agreement obligating parties to keep specified project or commercial information confidential, commonly signed before sharing tender documents or drawings.

---

## O

**On Hold (Project Status)**
A project status indicating work has been paused, as distinct from active or completed.

**Overhead**
Indirect project or company costs not tied to a specific work item — site establishment, supervision, insurance — typically allocated as a percentage in the cost plan.

**Owner's Engineer**
A technical representative appointed by the client to oversee construction on their behalf, reviewing progress, quality, and certifying payments.

**Occupancy Certificate**
A certificate issued by a local authority confirming a building complies with approved plans and is fit for occupation.

**Order of Precedence**
A clause in a contract specifying which document governs in case of conflict between multiple contract documents (drawings, specifications, BOQ, conditions of contract).

---

## P

**P&L (Profit & Loss)**
A financial statement summarizing revenue, costs, and resulting profit or loss for a project or company over a defined period.

**PPE (Personal Protective Equipment)**
Safety gear — helmets, harnesses, gloves, high-visibility vests — required on site, with compliance tracked as a percentage per site or crew.

**Procurement**
The end-to-end process of sourcing and acquiring materials and services — from indent, through RFQ and vendor comparison, to purchase order, receipt, and invoice matching.

**Production Batch**
A discrete quantity of a manufactured construction material tracked with its own recipe, material consumption, and quality/variance data.

**Professional Tax**
A state-level tax in India levied on salaried employees and professionals, deducted through payroll and remitted to the state government.

**Progress Claim**
A contractor's periodic request for payment based on work completed to date, supporting an RA bill or interim payment certificate.

**Provisional Sum**
An estimated allowance included in a BOQ for work or items not fully defined at contract signing, adjusted to actual cost once the scope is finalized.

**PF (Provident Fund)**
A statutory Indian retirement savings scheme requiring matched contributions from employer and employee, deducted through payroll.

**Performance Bond**
A guarantee, typically 5-10% of contract value, ensuring the client is compensated if the contractor fails to perform according to the contract.

**Petty Cash**
A small fund of cash kept on site or in office for minor, immediate expenses that don't warrant a full purchase order or bank transaction.

**Pre-Qualification**
A screening process evaluating a contractor's financial standing, technical capability, and track record before they are invited to bid on a tender.

**PWA (Progressive Web App)**
A web application built to behave like a native mobile app — installable, capable of offline use, and able to access device features like GPS and camera — used for SiteFlow's field-facing attendance and DPR tools.

---

## Q

**Quality Checklist**
A structured list of inspection points (often mapped to IS codes) that a work item must satisfy before being accepted as complete.

**Quantity Surveyor (QS)**
A professional responsible for measuring, costing, and valuing construction work, typically managing BOQs, variations, and interim payment valuations.

**Quotation (CRM)**
A formal price offer sent to a prospective client for a project or scope of work, typically the final stage of a CRM lead before contract signing.

**Quality Assurance vs. Quality Control**
Quality assurance is the set of planned processes designed to prevent defects before work happens; quality control is the inspection and testing that verifies completed work actually meets the standard.

---

## R

**RA Bill (Running Account Bill)**
A periodic bill raised by a subcontractor against work completed to date, net of previous payments, retention, and statutory deductions.

**Rate Analysis**
A detailed breakdown of the unit rate for a work item into its constituent material, labour, and equipment costs, used to justify or negotiate BOQ rates.

**Reconciliation**
The process of matching two sets of records (e.g., bank statement vs. cashbook, material issued vs. consumed) to confirm they agree and to explain any difference.

**Retention**
A percentage of each RA bill (commonly 5–10%) withheld by the client as security against defects, released partly on completion and partly after the defects liability period.

**RFI (Request for Information)**
A formal query raised by a contractor to the design team or client seeking clarification on drawings, specifications, or site conditions.

**RFQ (Request for Quotation)**
A document sent to multiple vendors inviting price and terms for a defined scope of materials or work, used to compare offers before issuing a purchase order.

**Rolling Lookahead**
See **Lookahead Schedule**.

**Rebar**
Steel reinforcement bars embedded in concrete to provide tensile strength, specified and quantified through the bar bending schedule.

**Receivables Ageing**
An analysis of outstanding client invoices grouped by how long they have been unpaid, used to prioritize collection efforts.

**Reverse Charge Mechanism**
A GST provision where the recipient of goods or services, rather than the supplier, is liable to pay the tax directly to the government, applicable to certain construction-related supplies.

**Risk Register**
A living document listing identified project risks, their likelihood and impact, and the assigned owner and mitigation plan for each.

---

## S

**S-Curve**
A graph plotting cumulative project progress or cost against time, used to compare planned vs. actual progress at a glance.

**Scope Creep**
The gradual, often unapproved expansion of a project's scope beyond what was originally contracted, typically without corresponding adjustment to time or budget.

**Snag List (Punch List)**
A list of minor defects or incomplete items identified during a pre-handover inspection, which must be rectified before final handover.

**Statutory Compliance**
The set of legally mandated labour welfare and taxation obligations a construction company must meet — PF, ESI, BOCW cess, TDS, professional tax.

**Sub-Contract**
An agreement where a main contractor engages a specialist third party to execute a defined portion of the overall project scope.

**Substantial Completion**
The stage at which a project is complete enough for its intended use, even if minor finishing items remain outstanding.

**Sunk Cost**
Money already spent on a project that cannot be recovered, which should not factor into forward-looking decisions about the project's continuation.

**Safety Induction**
A mandatory briefing given to every worker or visitor before entering a site for the first time, covering site-specific hazards, rules, and emergency procedures.

**Schedule of Rates (SOR)**
A pre-published list of standard unit rates for common work items, often issued by a government body or client organization, used as a benchmark or basis for BOQ pricing.

**Shop Drawing**
A detailed drawing prepared by a contractor or fabricator (based on design drawings) showing exactly how a component will be manufactured or installed, submitted for designer approval before fabrication.

**Site Instruction**
A formal written direction issued by the client's representative or consultant to the contractor on site, which may or may not carry cost or time implications.

**Stock Take**
A physical count of warehouse or site inventory conducted periodically to verify recorded stock levels match what is actually on hand.

---

## T

**Tally Prime Integration**
A connection exporting finance transactions (vendor bills, sales invoices, payments) from a project ERP as a Tally-importable voucher file.

**TDS (Tax Deducted at Source)**
An Indian tax mechanism requiring the payer to deduct a percentage of a payment upfront and remit it directly to the government on the payee's behalf.

**Tender**
A formal invitation for contractors to submit competitive bids for a defined scope of work, typically evaluated on price, technical capability, and track record.

**Three-Way Match**
A procurement control verifying a purchase order, goods receipt note, and vendor invoice agree on quantity and price before payment approval.

**Toolbox Talk**
A short, informal safety briefing held on site to cover hazards relevant to the work about to be performed.

**Turnkey Project**
A project delivered by a single contractor responsible for the entire scope from design through commissioning, handed over ready for use.

**Target Cost**
An agreed cost benchmark in certain contract types where savings or overruns against the target are shared between client and contractor per a pre-agreed formula.

**Temporary Works**
Structures or works needed to support construction but not forming part of the permanent finished building — scaffolding, shoring, formwork, site hoarding.

**Trial Balance**
A bookkeeping report listing all ledger account balances at a point in time, used to check that total debits equal total credits before preparing financial statements.

---

## U

**Unit Rate**
The price assigned to a single unit of a BOQ item, which when multiplied by quantity gives the item's total cost.

**Uplift (Cost Uplift)**
An additional percentage applied to a base rate or cost to account for site conditions, escalation, or contingency.

**Utilization Rate**
A measure of how much of an available resource (equipment, labour, warehouse capacity) is actively being used against its full capacity.

**Unbilled Revenue**
Revenue for work already completed but not yet invoiced to the client, recognized in project accounts ahead of the formal bill being raised.

---

## V

**Variance (Cost/Schedule)**
The difference between planned and actual cost or schedule performance at a given point in a project.

**Variation Order (VO)**
A formally approved change to the original contract scope, quantity, or specification, adjusting the contract value and/or schedule.

**Vendor Scorecard**
A recurring assessment of a vendor's performance across metrics like on-time delivery, quality rejection rate, and price competitiveness.

**Voucher (Payment)**
An internal document authorizing and recording a payment, typically requiring sign-off before funds are released.

**Value Engineering**
A systematic review of a design or specification to find lower-cost alternatives that deliver the same function and quality, without compromising performance.

**Vendor Master**
The central record of all approved vendors and their details (contact, bank account, tax registration, category) used across procurement and payment workflows.

---

## W

**Warehouse Inventory**
The stock of materials held at a project or central store, tracked by quantity, location, and movement.

**Warranty Period**
The period during which a manufacturer or contractor guarantees the performance of installed equipment or work, distinct from (and sometimes longer than) the defects liability period.

**WBS (Work Breakdown Structure)**
A hierarchical decomposition of a project into progressively smaller, manageable work packages, forming the structural basis for scheduling and budgeting.

**Work Order**
A formal instruction issued to a subcontractor or vendor authorizing a defined scope of work at an agreed price.

**Works Contract**
A contract involving both the supply of goods and the provision of services (as in most construction agreements), with specific tax treatment under Indian GST law.

**Wage Register**
A statutory record maintained by an employer listing wages paid to each worker, required under Indian labour law and often inspected during compliance audits.

**Warranty Bond**
A guarantee ensuring that a contractor or supplier will remedy defects that emerge during the warranty period, distinct from a performance bond covering the main contract.

**Weekly Timesheet**
A record of hours or days worked by an employee or worker over a week, used as the basis for wage calculation and payroll processing.

**Work in Progress (WIP)**
The value of work completed but not yet billed or recognized as final revenue, tracked to understand a project's true financial position at any point in time.

---

## X

**X-Bracing**
A structural bracing pattern using diagonal members crossing in an "X" shape, commonly used in scaffolding and steel frame structures for lateral stability.

---

## Y

**Yield (Material)**
The actual usable output obtained from a given quantity of raw material, used to assess wastage against the theoretical BOQ norm.

**Year-End Reconciliation**
The process of closing out and verifying all financial records — cashbook, ledgers, statutory dues — at the close of a financial year.

**Year-on-Year (YoY) Growth**
A comparison of a metric (revenue, order book, headcount) against the same period one year earlier, used to assess a company's growth trend independent of seasonal fluctuation.

---

## Z

**ZATCA E-Invoice**
An electronic invoice generated in the format mandated by Saudi Arabia's Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority for VAT compliance under its e-invoicing (FATOORAH) regulations.

**Zero Defect Handover**
A handover target where all identified snags have been closed out before the client takes possession, as opposed to a snag list carried past handover.

**Zero-Based Budgeting**
A budgeting approach where every expense must be justified from zero for each new period, rather than simply adjusting the previous period's budget.

**Zoning (Site)**
The division of a construction site into designated areas (material storage, labour camp, batching plant, active work zone) to organize logistics and safety.

---
